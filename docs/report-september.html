What actually moves validity at a fixed budget

Eighty-five training runs at one model size and one token budget, 186 sampling runs over them, ranked by the only measurement that separates them: how many distinct valid molecules come out of 10,000 attempts. Two changes survive. Validation loss separates nothing at all, and saying so is part of the result.

Fixing size and budget is what makes the study cheap: one run is five minutes on one H100, so a wave of twelve variants costs about twenty minutes. Validity is measured by rdkit on 10,000 sampled strings, which gives a binomial 95% interval of half a point, and that interval is badly misleading: seed-to-seed spread within one configuration reached eleven points for the best training change and nineteen for one of its combinations, so single-seed rows are read as suggestions and nothing here is called an effect without three seeds. Validation loss is not used to rank anything here: it saturates between 0.75 and 0.80 for 5M, 17M and 45M parameters alike, while their validity differs by a factor of three.

The changes worth keeping

These two are the whole positive result of eighty-five training runs. They are independent, one in decoding and one in training, and they compose: together they return about 3800 distinct valid molecules per 10,000 attempts against 617 for the original setup, with uniqueness above 99% and a length distribution that still looks like the corpus. Everything anyone would want to add on top, including four configurations that looked like winners on one seed each, turned out to be draws from the same wide distribution. That half of the report is the more useful one.

Where the timesteps come from

The original objective draws the diffusion time uniformly on the unit interval. Drawing it from a logit-normal instead, so most of the mass sits at intermediate noise and both extremes are rare, is the largest training-side effect in the study. It is also a narrow peak rather than a direction: mean 0 with standard deviation 1 is the best setting tried, and moving the centre either way, or widening it, costs three to nine points. Shifting it towards the data end, where the task is easy, is the worst of them, which rules out "fewer easy steps" as the explanation and points at the middle of the schedule as where the score is worth learning.

Argmax decoding at 300 solver steps in every row, against the same reference. The last row is the one to argue about: doubling the budget under this timestep distribution costs six points, where the same doubling under uniform timesteps gained four.

One knob at a time

Fifteen single-knob variants against the same reference, all decoded with argmax at 300 solver steps, one seed each. Read the small positives as candidates, not results: of the four that were followed up with a second seed, three did not survive. The masking rows are the most informative negatives: padding is the only signal on a fixed canvas that tells the model where a molecule ends, so taking it out of the loss or out of attention removes termination supervision and generation collapses.

Shaping the loss

Both terms of this objective, the token cross-entropy and the reconstruction error, are gated to run only on low-noise states, and every row here is argmax at 300 solver steps. That gate is the reason min-SNR weighting fails here: capping the weight of high signal-to-noise timesteps removes most of the token-level supervision. Raising the gate instead, so the terms see only the cleanest states, moves in the helpful direction.

Turning a trajectory into a string

Roughly nine in ten invalid outputs fail on brackets or ring digits rather than chemistry, and both are decidable from the string alone while decoding. Repairing them costs no training and is the single largest gain measured anywhere in this study. Yield is per attempt, not per non-empty string, because two of the modes can return nothing; length is over valid molecules only, against a corpus mean of 44.3 characters.

Closing what is open is the honest default: it preserves the length distribution and doubles the yield. The mixed mode chooses per string and gives almost four times the molecules, but the average one is 26 characters, so that number has to be quoted next to its length.

Both repair modes on the checkpoints that matter

Seven checkpoints decoded three ways, 100 solver steps throughout. Closing is the column to quote when the length distribution has to hold: on the cross-entropy configuration it keeps uniqueness at or above 99.5% and lands within six characters of the corpus mean of 44.3, on both seeds.

Both changes, on the same twelve checkpoints

Each checkpoint decoded twice, argmax and mixed repair, 100 solver steps both times. Unique valid is the count of distinct molecules per 10,000 attempts, which is what a generative model is actually judged on: the original setup returns 617.

The ranking is not the same under the two decoders. The sphere-corruption run is sixth on argmax and first once repaired, so a screening study that ranks on argmax alone can pick the wrong winner.

The solver

Sampling settings were swept on one fixed checkpoint. The stochastic sampler at full noise is already the best of them, the deterministic limit is a point and a half worse, and the step count barely matters for the reference while the logit-normal winner actually prefers the cheap end, 15.29% at 100 steps against 13.55% at 300. So generation runs at 100 steps where the config asked for 300.

Where the solver puts its steps

The grid was uniform in t. Redistributing the same hundred steps, keeping both endpoints, costs nothing to try, and the answer is that the original grid was already the right one.

Negative results, and why they are worth reporting

    Scale, held back deliberately

    Size and budget were frozen for this study, but the two curves that justify the choice are worth recording. Validity keeps climbing with budget and with size, and parameters buy more than passes: at a fixed 160 tokens per parameter, going from 5M to 45M is worth twelve points, while quadrupling the budget on the small model is worth seven. That last curve is not universal, though. Under logit-normal timesteps the same doubling from 80 to 160 tokens per parameter goes the other way, 13.55% down to 7.23%. Eighty and 161 passes over a 224k-molecule corpus are both a great deal of repetition, and the mid-noise-heavy schedule reaches the point where it hurts sooner.

    What a third seed does to a screening study

    Every row here is 100 solver steps and argmax decoding, so seeds are directly comparable. The reference is stable, a quarter of a point between its two seeds. The logit-normal configuration is not: four seeds give 15.29%, 14.20%, 7.18% and 18.08%. Its mean is 2.4 times the reference and its own spread is a factor of two and a half. So the timestep change raises the mean a lot and raises the variance with it, and that belongs in a paper as a property of the method rather than hidden behind a best-of-four number.

    The same variance dissolved every add-on in turn. Weighting padding at 0.6 read 26.15% on one seed and 7.46% on the next. The cross-entropy weight looked like a stabiliser on two seeds, 60.29% and 60.38% repaired, and on four its argmax range is 9.50-18.82%, exactly as wide as the plain configuration's. At this size one run resolves nothing below roughly ten points, twenty times wider than the half-point binomial interval that 10,000 samples suggests.

    Two attempts to reduce the spread failed. Quadrupling the warmup lowers the mean to 8.44% and keeps the spread; halving the peak learning rate moves neither. So the instability is not obviously an optimisation artefact, and finding its source is the open question this study ends on.
